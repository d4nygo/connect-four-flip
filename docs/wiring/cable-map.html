What changed for the breadboard

Everything plugs into the free side

From your photo, the side with 3V3, GND, D15 … D22, D23 has one free hole per pin. The VIN / D13 side is buried in the breadboard, so nothing connects there.

New pin plan

Columns 0–6 on D23, D22, D21, D19, D18, D5, D15. Servo on D4. Nothing else: no LED strip, no buzzer, no resistors.

Sensors take 5 V from the battery rail

VIN is on the buried side, so the sensors share the battery's + and − rails with the servo. The ESP32 still runs from USB. One wire joins its GND to the − rail.

All cables reach the breadboard from the back

The back sensors' wires cross to the front through a hole near the bar's right end. Everything leaves through the right upright, gets joined on its outside face, and 9 wires go down its channel. The servo cable goes down the left one. Both leave the foot sockets through a notch and run across the base.

The breadboard, hole by hole

Seen from above, USB plug on the left. Only the left half of the breadboard is shown; nothing goes on the right half. Each coloured line starts in the hole you plug that wire into.

Hole numbers on this page are counted along the breadboard from the ESP32's USB end: hole 1 is level with the 3V3 pin, hole 2 with GND, up to hole 15 with D23. Your breadboard's printed numbers may run the other way, so count from the ESP32 instead of reading them.

The power rails are cut in the middle. On your breadboard the red and blue lines stop halfway along. Keep every + and − connection on the left half, as drawn, or the battery won't reach anything.

Connect it, step by step

Do the steps in this order. Each one ends with a check, so a mistake shows up straight away and not at the end. Keep the battery switch off and USB unplugged whenever you add or move a wire.

  1. Gather everything

    • ESP32, breadboard, 14 A3144 sensors, servo with its horn, 4×AA holder with a switch and 4 AA batteries (rechargeable or normal, both work now).
    • Thin stranded wire, 28–30 AWG, in 9 colours if you can: red, black, blue, green, yellow, purple, white, grey, cyan. About 8 m in total. A rainbow ribbon cable that you peel apart works well.
    • Male-to-male jumper wires from the kit (for the breadboard ends), heat-shrink tube (or electrical tape), a soldering iron if you can borrow one, a multimeter, a marker, small pieces of tape.

  2. Put the ESP32 in the breadboard

    Exactly like your photo: USB end overhanging the left end, the 3V3 … D23 row towards you. That row sits in row h and leaves row j free in front of each pin. The VIN … EN row is buried in row a.

    Check: plug in USB and upload Blink. The blue LED blinks. Unplug USB.

  3. Ground link (black jumper)

    Row j hole 2 (in front of GND) to the blue − rail, hole 2.

  4. Battery holder

    Switch off. Red lead to the red + rail at hole 30, black lead to the blue − rail at hole 29. Both are on the left half of the rails, left of the cut in the middle. If the leads are bare wire, twist each onto a male jumper and tape or heat-shrink it.

    Check: switch on, multimeter between + and − rail: 4.8–6.4 V. Switch off.

  5. Servo, on the table first

    The servo plug has 3 holes. Push a male-to-male jumper into each: orange → row j hole 5 (D4), red → + rail hole 0 (the hole just left of the 3V3 pin), brown → − rail hole 1.

    Check: USB in, battery on, run the ESP32Servo "Sweep" example on pin 4 with small angles. The servo moves and the ESP32 does not restart. Then battery off, USB out, and fix the servo into the left upright.

  6. Prepare the sensor wires

    • For each face of the bar (front and back): one red and one black wire, 25 cm, and 7 signal wires, one per column, in the column colours: column 0 blue, 1 green, 2 yellow, 3 purple, 4 white, 5 grey, 6 cyan. Cut each signal wire to (distance from its column to the right end of the bar) + 5 cm: about 25, 22, 19, 16, 14, 11 and 8 cm.
    • For the bundle: 9 wires of 60 cm: red, black and the 7 column colours.

  7. Fit and wire the front sensors

    • Each sensor goes in its pocket with the printed, flat face towards the coin slot. The 3D thread's guide shows how it sits.
    • The three legs hang straight down through the small leg channel under the sensor into the wire trench, then bend outwards into the trench. With the printed face towards the slot, the leg order is 1 (5 V), 2 (GND), 3 (signal) from left to right as seen from inside the slot. Mark leg 1 with the marker before you bend, so you don't lose track.
    • Lay the red and black wires along the wire trench at the bottom of the front face (about 8.5 mm tall, 4 mm deep), from column 0 to past the right end. At each column, strip 3 mm of insulation off the red wire and solder leg 1 to it. Do the same for leg 2 on the black wire. Trim the leg, then cover each joint with a bit of tape.
    • Solder each column's signal wire to that sensor's leg 3, cover it, and run it in the trench to the right end.
    • Check: with the multimeter on beep, red wire to each leg 1, black to each leg 2, and no beep between red and black.

  8. Back sensors

    Exactly the same on the back face, with its own red, black and 7 signals. Same column colours. At the right end, push all 9 back wires through the 7 mm hole at x = 99.5 (just left of the bar's right end) so they come out in the front trench, next to the front wires. Do this before soldering the last joints, it's easier with loose ends.

  9. Splice point (outside face of the right upright)

    • All 18 wires leave the front trench at the right end, go through the slot in the right ear's front cheek and the hole through the right upright, and come out on its outside face. They land in the splice bay, a 5 mm deep pocket on the outside face that links the hole to the cable channel. Do the joints there.
    • Join, twist and solder, then heat-shrink each joint: front red + back red + bundle red, front black + back black + bundle black, and for each column front signal + back signal + bundle wire of the same colour. That makes 9 joints.
    • Slide one big piece of heat-shrink or tape over the whole bunch so nothing can touch.

  10. Run the bundle

    The 9 wires go from the splice into the cable channel along the back edge of the right upright's outside face (6 mm wide, 5 mm deep). Press them in and add tape at the top and the bottom. At the bottom they leave through the notch in the foot socket, then run across the back of the base (tape every 5–10 cm) to the front of the breadboard.

  11. Plug the bundle into the breadboard

    Give every bundle wire a male jumper end (solder, or twist and heat-shrink), then plug it in:

    • Blue (column 0) → row j hole 15 (D23) · green (1) → hole 14 (D22) · yellow (2) → hole 11 (D21) · purple (3) → hole 10 (D19) · white (4) → hole 9 (D18) · grey (5) → hole 8 (D5) · cyan (6) → hole 3 (D15)
    • Red → + rail hole 17 · black → − rail hole 18

  12. Servo cable

    From the servo, into the left upright's channel, out through the foot socket's notch, across the base to the breadboard. The jumpers from step 6 stay as they are. If it doesn't reach, add three female-to-male jumpers as an extension.

  13. Final check before power

    • Multimeter on beep: no beep between the + and − rails. If it beeps, something is shorted; find it before going on.
    • Each row j hole beeps to the matching sensor's leg 3 on both faces.
    • Nothing is plugged into the buried VIN … EN side, and nothing is on the right half of the rails.

  14. Power up and test

    USB in first, then battery on. Run the IDE's DigitalReadSerial example with INPUT_PULLUP on each column pin in turn: it prints 1, and 0 when a magnet coin drops past either sensor. Switch the battery off before you unplug USB or upload new code.

Inside the entry bar

Each column has a sensor in the front wall and one in the back wall. The legs go down a small leg channel into the wire trench at the bottom of the bar, front face and back face separately.

  • Front face: a red 5 V wire and a black GND wire run the whole length. Every front sensor's leg 1 joins the red, leg 2 the black. Each leg 3 (signal) gets its own thin wire to the right end.
  • Back face: exactly the same, a separate red, black and 7 signals. They cross to the front trench through the 6 mm hole near the right end.
  • Splice point (the splice bay, a 5 mm deep pocket on the right upright's outside face between the wire hole and the cable channel): front red + back red are joined, front black + back black are joined, and each column's front signal is joined to its back signal. That's the "two sensors share one wire" trick. Out of the splice come 9 wires.

Things to check

  • Which side is free? I read your photo as the 3V3 … D23 side being free. If it's actually the VIN … D13 side, tell me and I'll swap the plan.
  • Thin wire for the bar. The trench is 8.5 × 4 mm and the cross hole is 7 mm. Use 26–28 AWG stranded wire on the bar and down the channel.
  • Through the right upright. 18 thin wires go through the upright's 10 × 7.5 mm hole into the splice bay. That fits thin wire comfortably; if it's still tight, splice inside the ear's cheek instead, so only 9 go through.
  • Servo lead: the route is about 30 cm, and the servo's own cable is about 30 cm. If it's short, add three female-to-male jumpers.
  • Battery: the sensors need at least 4.5 V and the servo at most 7.2 V. Four AAs fit: normal ones give about 6 V, rechargeable ones about 4.8–5.2 V. With rechargeables, recharge if coins start being missed.

Why D5 is fine now: a start-up pin only causes trouble if something holds it at the wrong level when the board powers on. Our sensors can only pull a wire down, and only while a magnet sits right in front of them. With no resistors on the board, nothing holds D5 up or down at power-on, so it behaves like a normal pin. Just don't switch on with a coin stuck in column 5. The same goes for D15 (column 6): if it is pulled down at power-on, the board only skips printing its start-up messages. Why not TX2/RX2 (GPIO 17/16): on some ESP32 boards (WROVER modules) these two pins are wired to extra memory inside the module and must not be used. Ours is probably a WROOM, where they would work, but D15 is safe on every version, so we follow your friend's advice. Why not D2: the board's blue LED hangs on it and can pull the signal down, so a sensor might look "on" all the time.
